### Fixed

- On a Mac, Linux apps no longer show **"… is damaged and can't be opened"** after you install Newport from the website. Newport now signs each app it creates, and replaces an app it created earlier the next time you open it.
- Turning **Open as separate apps** on or off moves the Linux apps that are already open, without closing them. Before, the setting applied only after every app had been closed.
- A Linux app kept in the Dock opens when you click it, even when Newport isn't running or the app isn't open: Newport starts in the background and opens it. Before, nothing opened, or the app said to open it from Newport.
- Opening a Linux app that is already running, such as 1Password, no longer warns that it **closed without opening a window** when the running app shows its window instead.
- Linux apps that are already running when Newport connects get their own name and icon in the Dock and the taskbar. Before, some showed their file name, such as `chromium-browser`, with a letter tile.

### Improved

- On a Mac, each app a server lists becomes a Mac app with its own name and icon as soon as you open **Remote apps** for the server, ready to keep in the Dock. Newport keeps them up to date as the server's apps change, and removes them when you remove the server.

### Before you install

On macOS, you need Apple silicon and macOS 14 or later. Windows versions are experimental and not code-signed yet. Linux apps need Newport Pro or a free trial.
